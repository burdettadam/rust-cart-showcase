    03 / UNDER THE HOOD

    One set of rules.
    Two places to run.

    The shopping-cart library runs natively in Rust and in your browser through WebAssembly. This companion gives its behavior a visible, interactive surface.

    RustWebAssemblyStatic HTML + JS
    01 What does Rust own?+

    Cart state, quantity validation, exact price parsing, checked arithmetic and totals. JavaScript sends the raw price response to the WASM module and displays the decimal strings it returns. No money calculations happen in JavaScript.

    02 Why not use floating-point money?+

    The library works in checked integer cents. Tax is 12.5% of the full subtotal, rounded upward once. A price with significant fractions of a cent is rejected rather than silently rounded. Prices must use ordinary decimal JSON number notation; exponent notation is unsupported.

    03 What happens when something fails?+

    A rejected price, invalid quantity, failed request or arithmetic overflow leaves the last accepted cart state intact. Requests have a five-second budget and a 16 KiB response limit. Resetting the cart or switching price sources cancels a pending request and prevents late results from changing the new cart.

    04 What are the boundaries of this demo?+

    This is an in-memory cart: no checkout, stock reservation or saved basket. Refreshing starts over. Adding an existing product combines quantity and reprices all its units. The library-only challenge remains separate from this showcase.

    05 How was AI involved?+

    The developer directs the design and makes acceptance decisions. ChatGPT produced the implementation and test drafts and provides technical analysis. Automated tests and independent AI reviews supply evidence; they do not replace the developer's own review or judgment.
